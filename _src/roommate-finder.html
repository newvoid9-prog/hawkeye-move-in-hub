How the roommate search works at Iowa

First-year students apply for housing through University Housing and Dining after they are admitted and pay the $100 housing application fee. Once your application is in, you can search for other applicants and send a roommate request. A request only counts when it is mutual: both of you have to request each other before room assignments are made. If you do not request anyone, you are matched with a roommate based on your housing preferences.

The official roommate search is the only place a match becomes binding. Everything else on this page is about finding the right person to type into it.

"Class of 2031" accounts post intro slides of admitted students, and most roommate pairs start in those comment sections and DMs. Use them to find people, then use the university's search to make it official.

The 12 questions to ask before you commit

A good roommate is not your best friend. It is someone whose daily habits do not collide with yours. Ask these before you send a request:

  1. What time do you usually go to bed on a school night, and on a weekend?
  2. Do you study in your room or somewhere else?
  3. How clean is clean enough?
  4. How do you feel about guests staying over?
  5. Are you an early class person or an afternoon class person?
  6. Do you want the room to be a social spot or a quiet spot?
  7. Music or TV out loud, or headphones?
  8. Which halls are you putting first, and why?
  9. Will most of your classes be on the east side or the west side?
  10. What are you bringing: fridge, TV, rug, microwave?
  11. How do you like to handle a problem, text it or talk it out?
  12. What is one thing a past roommate or sibling would complain about?

Then get on a video call. Ten minutes of talking tells you more than a week of messages.

Red flags in a roommate match

  • They only want a hall you have ranked last.
  • Every answer is "I'm super chill." Nobody is chill about everything.
  • They push for a decision before you have talked once outside of DMs.
  • They will not answer the money questions, like splitting a fridge.

When going random is the smarter call

Random roommates work out more often than incoming students expect, and they are a built-in way to meet someone outside your existing circle. Go random if you have not clicked with anyone on a real call, or if a pairing would force you into a hall you do not want. Picking the right hall usually matters more than picking a stranger from a comment section, so compare halls on our University of Iowa dorms ranked list first.

Before you request anyone: agree on your top three halls together. A mutual request still has to land in a hall with space for both of you.

What to do after you match

Split the big items so you do not end up with two fridges, follow each other on the class pages, and pick a move-in time.
