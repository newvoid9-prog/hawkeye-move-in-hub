Residence halls

University of Iowa dorms ranked, from worst to best

Iowa has ten residence halls for first-year students, and they are not equal. We ranked every one on location, room quality, dining, and how easy it is to meet people.

Key takeaways

  • Best overall: Catlett, the newest and largest hall, with its own dining hall on the east side.
  • Most social: Burge, with 922 residents and a dining hall in the building.
  • Best value: Mayflower, if you can live with a bus ride, because its suites have kitchens.
  • Only three halls hold the all-you-care-to-eat dining halls: Burge, Catlett, and Hillcrest.

How we ranked the halls

The Iowa River splits campus in two. The east side holds most classroom buildings, downtown Iowa City, and the Pentacrest. The west side holds the hospitals, the health sciences, and Kinnick Stadium. Where your hall sits decides how long your walk to class is, so location carries the most weight. Room quality, a dining hall in the building, and social energy fill out the rest. Resident counts and amenities come from University Housing and Dining; the order is our opinion.

The ranking, number 10 to number 1

  1. Slater Hall

    • West side
    • 489 residents
    • Double $8,749

    A west side high-rise with a long walk to most east side classes. It has a music practice room and sits next to the cultural houses, but the nearest dining hall is Hillcrest.

  2. Rienow Hall

    • West side
    • 488 residents
    • Double $8,749

    Slater's twin tower next door, close to the Field House. Same layout, same walk to Hillcrest for meals, same verdict.

  3. Mayflower Hall

    • East side, north
    • 810 residents
    • Suites with kitchens
    • Double $8,749

    Suite-style rooms with an in-suite kitchen and bathroom, a fitness center, and the Mayflower Market convenience store. The catch is location: it sits north along the river, far from the center of campus. More on Mayflower below.

  4. Stanley Hall

    • East side
    • 354 residents
    • Double $8,749

    Small, quiet, and connected to Currier, with a music practice room. Burge and Catlett are the nearest dining halls. A solid pick if you want calm over chaos.

  5. Hillcrest Hall

    • West side
    • 823 residents
    • Dining hall inside
    • Quad $6,460

    The anchor of the west side, with Hillcrest Market Place and Grand Avenue Market in the building, a fitness center, and the health sciences campus next door. It also has the cheapest room on campus, the Hillcrest quad. Far from everything else.

  6. Daum Hall

    • East side
    • 344 residents
    • Double $8,749

    The smallest first-year hall, but on the east side and a short walk from almost everything. Location carries it.

  7. Burge Hall

    • East side
    • 922 residents
    • Dining hall inside
    • Double $8,749

    Burge Market Place and Clinton Street Market are in the building, and so are 922 first-years. If you want to meet people fast, this is it.

  8. Petersen Hall

    • West side
    • 501 residents
    • Double $10,048

    The newer west side hall, with modern rooms, a neighborhood learning commons, and Yella's at Black's Gold Grill downstairs. Hillcrest Market Place is next door. Its doubles cost $1,299 more than a standard double.

  9. Currier Hall

    • East side
    • 628 residents
    • Double $8,749

    Historic and good looking, with a fitness center, computer lab, and community room, a short walk to both Burge and Catlett for meals. Great location at the standard price. The safe great pick.

  10. Catlett Hall

    • East side
    • 1,049 residents
    • Dining hall inside
    • Double $10,048

    The newest and largest hall on campus, on the east bank of the river with Catlett Market Place in the building and a short walk to class. It costs more, and it is still the one everyone requests first.

Mayflower Hall, explained

More people search for Mayflower Hall than any other Iowa dorm, and it is the hall students argue about most. Here is the honest case for and against.

Why students love it

  • Suite-style rooms with an in-suite kitchen and bathroom, so no shared hall bathroom.
  • A fitness center and the Mayflower Market convenience store in the building.
  • Because the suites have kitchens, residents can usually choose the smaller Block 50 meal plan ($1,750 a year) instead of a $4,425 or higher plan. Confirm eligibility with University Dining.

Why it ranks eighth here

  • It sits north of the main campus along the river, so most students take the bus to class.
  • There is no all-you-care-to-eat dining hall inside, only the market.
  • It is easier to stay in your suite and harder to meet people across the hall.

The math: a Mayflower double plus Block 50 is about $10,499 for the year, which can make it one of the cheapest ways to live on campus. If budget matters more than a short walk, move Mayflower up your list.

The best Iowa dorm for each kind of student

  • Most social: Burge.
  • Best rooms: Catlett, then Petersen.
  • Health sciences and nursing: Hillcrest, then Petersen.
  • Quietest: Stanley or Daum.
  • Own bathroom and a kitchen: Mayflower.
  • Tightest budget: a Hillcrest quad or Mayflower with Block 50.

Parklawn Hall is apartment-style housing for returning students only, so it is left out of this first-year ranking. Once you have a shortlist, find someone to share it with using our University of Iowa roommate finder guide.
