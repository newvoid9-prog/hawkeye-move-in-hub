Housing step one

University of Iowa roommate finder: how to find a roommate you will actually like

Most admitted Hawkeyes have to name a roommate before they have met anyone in their class. This page walks through where to look, how to vet a match, and how to decide if random is the better bet.

How the roommate search works at Iowa

First-year students apply for housing through University Housing and Dining after they are admitted and pay the housing application fee. Once your application is in, you can search for other applicants and send a roommate request. A request only counts when it is mutual: both of you have to request each other before room assignments are made. If you do not request anyone, you are matched with a roommate based on your housing preferences.

The official roommate search is the only place a match actually becomes binding. Everything else on this page is about finding the right person to type into it.

Where admitted Hawkeyes actually find roommates

  • Class Instagram pages. "Class of 2031" accounts post intro slides of admitted students, and most roommate pairs start in those comment sections and DMs.
  • The university's own search. Filter by hall preference and lifestyle answers. Fewer people, but every one of them has already applied.
  • Roommate apps and group chats. Matching apps and class group chats are busy in spring. Useful for volume, noisy for quality.
  • Your own high school. Rooming with a friend from home is the safest pick and the one that teaches you the least. Plenty of people do it anyway, and that is fine.

The 12 questions to ask before you commit

A good roommate is not your best friend. It is someone whose daily habits do not collide with yours. Ask these before you send a request:

  1. What time do you usually go to bed on a school night, and on a weekend?
  2. Do you study in your room or somewhere else?
  3. How clean is clean enough?
  4. How do you feel about guests staying over?
  5. Are you an early class person or an afternoon class person?
  6. Do you want the room to be a social spot or a quiet spot?
  7. Music or TV out loud, or headphones?
  8. Which halls are you putting first, and why?
  9. Are you on an east side or west side schedule (your major's buildings)?
  10. What are you bringing: fridge, TV, rug, microwave?
  11. How do you like to handle a problem, text it or talk it out?
  12. What is one thing a past roommate or sibling would complain about?

Then get on a video call. Ten minutes of talking tells you more than a week of messages.

Red flags in a roommate match

  • They only want to pick a hall you have ranked last.
  • Every answer is "I'm super chill." Nobody is chill about everything.
  • They push for a decision before you have talked once outside of DMs.

When going random is the smarter call

Random roommates work out more often than incoming students expect, and they are a built-in way to meet someone outside your existing circle. Go random if you have not found someone you clicked with on a real call, or if a pairing would force you into a hall you do not want. Picking the right hall usually matters more than picking a stranger from a comment section. Compare halls on our Iowa dorms ranked page before you decide.

Before you request anyone: agree on your top three halls together, because a mutual request still has to land in a hall with space for both of you.

What to do after you match

Split the big items so you do not end up with two fridges, follow each other on the class pages, and decide on a move-in time. Then start meeting the rest of your floor. Our guide on how to make friends before college starts covers the next step.
