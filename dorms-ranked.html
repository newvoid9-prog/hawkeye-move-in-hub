Residence halls

University of Iowa dorms ranked, from worst to best

Iowa has ten residence halls for first-year students, and they are not equal. We ranked every one on four things: location, room quality, dining, and how easy it is to meet people.

How we ranked the halls

The Iowa River splits campus in two. The east side holds most classroom buildings, downtown Iowa City, and the Pentacrest. The west side holds the hospitals, the health sciences, and Kinnick Stadium. Where your hall sits decides how long your walk to class is, so location carries the most weight here. Room quality, an attached dining hall, and social energy fill out the rest.

  1. Slater HallA west side high-rise with some of the smallest rooms on campus and a long walk to most classes. It is the bottom of almost every list for a reason.
  2. Rienow HallSlater's twin tower next door. Same layout, same walk, same verdict.
  3. Mayflower HallSuite-style rooms with their own bathroom and a kitchenette, which is a real perk. The catch is location: it sits north along the river, far from the center of campus, and you will be riding the bus.
  4. Stanley HallSmall, quiet, and connected to Currier's dining. A solid pick if you want calm over chaos.
  5. Hillcrest HallThe anchor of the west side, with its own dining hall and the best location on campus for nursing, pre-med, and health sciences students. Far from everything else.
  6. Daum HallOlder and on the small side, but on the east side and a short walk from almost everything. Location carries it.
  7. Burge HallThe biggest and loudest hall, with dining in the building and the most first-year traffic on campus. If you want to meet people fast, this is it.
  8. Petersen HallThe newest hall on the west side, with modern rooms and lounges that feel more like a hotel. It costs more than a standard double, and it shows.
  9. Currier HallHistoric, good looking, east side, and attached to its own dining hall. The safe great pick.
  10. Catlett HallThe newest hall on campus, on the east bank of the river with views to match, a dining hall, and a short walk to class. The one everyone requests first.

Best Iowa dorm for each kind of student

  • Most social: Burge.
  • Best rooms: Catlett and Petersen.
  • Health sciences majors: Hillcrest, then Petersen.
  • Quietest: Stanley.
  • Want your own bathroom: Mayflower, if you can live with the bus.

Ranking is only half of it. Catlett and Petersen doubles cost more than a standard double elsewhere. See the full breakdown on our University of Iowa dorm costs page before you lock in your top three.

Which halls are not on this list

Parklawn Hall is apartment-style housing aimed at returning students, so it is left out of a first-year ranking. If you are searching for a roommate to share one of the halls above, start with our University of Iowa roommate finder guide.
